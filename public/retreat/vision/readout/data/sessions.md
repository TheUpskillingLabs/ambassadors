# What was said: the Oct 3 retreat, session by session

Summaries of the day, written from recordings. Quotes are word for word, with
obvious transcription errors fixed in [brackets], and credited by role, not
name. Spending, legal and staffing detail from the DC session is left out on
purpose. Two sessions weren't recorded: Explore our problems for Q4 (its
boards are in cards.csv) and the closing circle.

## State of the Labs

Ten months in, the board looked back on what the Labs has built. It started as
an idea in summer 2025. Since then there have been three build cycles: health
systems in January, climate and energy in April, and civics and elections now,
with a showcase on October 13. The numbers on the day:

- More than 1,600 event sign-ups and 1,300 attendees, across 100+ events
- More than 1,200 followers on LinkedIn and 280 people active in Slack
- About 100 Upskillers in 18 project teams, all of which shipped working prototypes
- More than 30 volunteers, organizers, mentors and advisors
- $70,000 in under ten months, from grants and from philanthropic donations
  by people in the community

> "In under a year, we have something really real and we have something
> unique." (A board member)

> "…a safe space where you can ask question[s] and no question really is a
> dumb question." (A board member)

## Welcome and check-in

Everyone shared a moment they were proud of this year and one word for the
quarter ahead. Four themes came up:

- **"I can build now."** Several people learned to vibe code this year and
  shipped real things: a prototype with a deployed backend, a working tool
  shown to peers in their field, and a ten-year-old client tool rebuilt.
- **Confidence and belonging during a job search.** For more than one person,
  the community made a long search feel less isolating.
- **Saying yes.** Saying yes to joining, to leading, to getting on a stage.
- **Consistency.** People were struck that a volunteer organization shows up
  week in, week out.

The words: opportunity, bullish, belonging, community (twice), curious,
confidence, expansive, thought-partner-y, possibility, money, hope, impact,
revolutionary, yes.

> "In a time when there's so much disruption in technology [that] constantly
> wants us to sort of isolate… what we actually need to use the technology
> well… is community." (A volunteer)

## Where we're headed

Q4 is about laying the foundation for 2027: "This quarter lets us say yes."

1. There's no public build cycle this quarter. Instead, the Labs runs an
   internal cycle on itself, to improve the cycle and the organization and to
   give volunteers a break.
2. Public workshops keep running, virtual and in person. They lead into the
   January cycle.
3. The Labs delivers on its first funding commitments, and delivers them well.
   For a young organization, they're the first proof points.
4. In 2027: DC becomes its own chapter with local leaders, the Labs raises
   money for a small full-time team, and it grows into new cities.

> "What we can accomplish in these next three months is going to be what lets
> us say yes to more opportunities next year." (A board member)

> "We are in a space where we struggle to describe ourselves to people because
> we're doing something new… We're not fitting a model of how they already
> think about things." (A board member)

## Sketch the moonshot

The facilitator set up the exercise with a history:

- The pilot: about 50 people in DC helping each other through layoffs and new
  technology, which the founders saw as a bellwether for other cities.
- Cheap printing, then the Junto: twelve people in Philadelphia who started the
  first lending library. That library served as the de facto Library of
  Congress until 1800.
- Kennedy choosing a goal far enough out to reset the race.
- AI has reset the race again, and building is now cheap.

> "Nobody knows what's going on. And now building is super cheap." (The facilitator)

> "What do we wanna say the world is gonna say we did to help navigate through
> this topsy-turvy time?" (The facilitator)

In the table discussions, people named near peers including civic tech
brigades ("the brigade being the actual unit"), community college networks,
4-H and the cooperative extension service, and state workforce efforts. People
kept reaching for a number ("We need numbers").

Three groups read their headlines aloud before time ran out. Two explained
theirs in terms the written headline left out:

> "…keeping true to our main focus, which is helping local communities and
> individuals to grow their skills, grow their confidence, and build
> community." (Group B, on "100+ governments + 10,000,000 Upskillers")

> "We believe in a future of work that's still human… that system is something
> that other people find value in… such that we're actually a new…
> institution." (Group D)

## Our value prop & messaging

Two frames set up the exercise:

- **Self-determination theory.** People are motivated when three needs are met:
  competence, autonomy and relatedness. The Labs maps these to Learn, Build and
  Share.
- **Sinek's golden circle.** Start with why someone would show up, then how
  it's different, then what they get.

Each person filled a grid for one persona, an Upskiller or a volunteer, in
2027. Then five small groups reported back. The three volunteer groups aren't
on the walls in cards.csv, so this is the only record of them.

> "The fact that the pods and the projects are relatively self-organizing is
> meant to enhance that sense of autonomy… not prescribing what people work
> on, but instead giving them a system to find what to work on." (A facilitator)

> "We saw a different type of upskiller this last build cycle. So the
> persona… we were building for past build cycles did not necessarily fit."
> (A facilitator)

**Upskillers: mid-career pivoters.** Skill building, "building a circle" for a
career and for community, and learning by doing.

> "There was the learning that happened in the sessions, and then it was when
> we applied it to our own… daily life, own workspace, that was where… a lot
> of the learning actually happened."

**Upskillers: curious, problem-solving.** A format that isn't a lecture, proof
of work to show employers, and sharing for status, relationships and fun. The
output is LinkedIn posts, building out loud, a résumé and a portfolio.

> "I paid $2,500 for [an] AI course last year. It sucked. I learned nothing. I
> learned by building." (An Upskiller)

**Volunteers: cycle alumni going further.** More scaffolding without
constraining people, contribution metrics and a public dashboard, and
spotlighting volunteers on social media:

> "…so that way their skills are being verif[ied], their publicly available
> proof, but also just to show that we care."

**Volunteers: mentors and community operators.** Experienced people who want
to be of service and to see the change through a new lens.

> "This is sort of like a playground for me… I've always had scaffolding that
> I've wanted to build in organizations, but I just couldn't cut through the
> bureaucracy to do it." (A volunteer mentor)

> "I say all the time, the Upskilling Labs is infectious… That is a function of
> sharing." (A volunteer mentor)

> "You can't learn through the struggle if you don't have a good group of
> people to do it with." (A volunteer mentor)

**Volunteers in 2027.** Lifelong learning, leadership and local relevance.
Building means time-boxed experiments on real problems. Sharing means
confidence, accountability and peer learning, "and then we get to propose our
own topics."

## Building DC's founding team

The board shared drafts of what a DC volunteer leadership committee would own
and how its first members would be chosen. The board decides; the community
informs. What the room heard:

- It's a committee inside the Upskilling Labs, not a separate organization.
  "We have to earn the right" to spin out.
- It has at least three people (a lead and two officers), and probably no more
  than five.
- It covers the DMV, not just the District.
- In 2027 it owns community, volunteer recruitment, local marketing and finding
  local partners, and it can pilot its own ideas, such as build nights.
- Before the committee takes something on, the board wants to have worked
  through some of its challenges first, so the committee starts from a model
  instead of from scratch.
- The room spotted a gap: communications isn't on the draft. What's local and
  what's national for comms still needs to be worked out.
- Models to learn from: the cooperative extension service and 4-H, the YMCA,
  Scouting's chartered host institutions, and open source foundations like
  Apache.

> "We're innovating in how we put these pieces together, but we're not coming
> up with something wholly new." (An advisor)

> "Slightly less building the plane, a little bit more flying it." (A board
> member, on what the internal cycle should do for future chapter leaders)
