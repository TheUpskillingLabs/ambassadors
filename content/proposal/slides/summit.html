The summit · Library of Congress · Proposed

Once a year, go to Washington to show. Never to ask.

The exhibition

A science fair in the Jefferson Building. Members find their teams.

Office visits

Each team, one afternoon, every member whose district it touches.

The report

One hour. One speaker. The numbers.

The pinning

Sponsors pin. Members watch. Nobody endorses anything.

The handover

The work goes into the Library’s collection. Then everybody goes home.

How I’d get there

  • Now: a Library of Congress advisor on our council.
  • Year one: first quarterly. A small demo day on the Hill. The deposit deal.
  • The summit when there’s enough to show. Not before.
  • Two former members as co-chairs. One from each party.

Left out on purpose

Asks. A policy platform. Panels with lobbyists. Logos from companies that sell to government.

The things we leave out are why they come back.

The Labs · A proposal · Keep it close

15

Talk track. Timing: late March, when the Hill is used to fly-ins, or National Library Week in April. If the Library declines in year one: the Rayburn foyer or the Kennedy Caucus Room, with the accession by deposit. Twelve months is possible, not the plan; a thin first summit is worse than a late one because Washington only keeps its first impression. No co-chair names or states on paper until both have said yes. The office visit is the hour most likely to drift into an ask; the team lead's brief is one sentence: show, answer, leave.
