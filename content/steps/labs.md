## What we are

It grew out of a fall 2025 pilot run by DC Public Library and Levy, a strategic design firm. The pilot's alums founded The Labs in January 2026. It's nonpartisan, and it isn't a school or a bootcamp. Most people start at a workshop:

## Why it needs you

### You found your way through.

Work changed under your feet, and you didn't face it alone. Free workshops by people who do the work for a living, and by Upskillers who learned it last cycle. A Build Cycle, fast and on a team, or your own project at your own pace. Whatever you got out of it, you did the getting. The Labs just made room. Now you're the one who knows the way.

### Be the one who says “come with me.”

A former colleague. A neighbor. Someone figuring out what's next. They don't need a pitch. They need someone they trust to say it. That's the whole job, and you're already good at it.

### Be how it reaches the next neighborhood.

The Labs isn't a place, and it isn't much of an organization yet: a year old, run by volunteers. It's something a region picks up and makes its own, the way Greater Washington did. It grew the way it started, with alums who couldn't keep it to themselves. If it's going to reach other regions, and other neighborhoods here, that's the only way it will.

### Be early to something built to last. Bring someone.

The Labs is a year old, volunteer-run, and growing. We hope it becomes a normal part of American life, the way scouting and the YMCA did: something every region has, that neighbors run, that outlasts the people who started it. That's a lot to hope for. It only happens if people build it early. And the future of work gets built either way. We want you competitive for the jobs coming, and nobody knows what those are. The jobs that are mostly labor are being automated. What's left is work and action: what people make together that lasts, and what they do together in public. So The Labs is built to shift as the tech and the world shift, in the open, one Build Cycle at a time. Maybe to lead a shift or two. If a few people build that future, the rest of us live in it. If neighbors build it too, everyone has a hand in it. Every person you bring is one more pair of hands. Yours are among the first.

## Your job

1. **Ask** someone what they're working toward.
2. **Share** 30 seconds of your own story.
3. **Invite** them to one dated next step.

You don't sell, and you don't collect contact details. People Join The Labs themselves at theupskillinglabs.org.
