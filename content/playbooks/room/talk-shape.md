summary: "Six beats, one per slide. The question gets the room involved, your story makes it personal, the one-sentence description and proof make it concrete, and the ask gives them something to do before they leave."
why: "Starting with the audience and ending with an action is what moves people. Everything in the middle earns the ask."
shape:
  - "Question (15 sec): one show-of-hands question."
  - "Story (30 sec): where you were, what happened, what changed."
  - "What The Labs is (30 sec): one sentence, then the ladder."
  - "Proof (15 sec): one stat or one quote."
  - "Ask (30 sec): the next date and what to expect."
  - "Join (rest): the QR stays up for Q&A."
examples:
  - "Question: \"Who here has changed jobs, or thought about it, in the last two years?\""
  - "Proof: \"More than 300 people have come through our programs since January.\""
  - "Ask: \"Thursday the 14th, 6 pm, right here. Bring a laptop if you have one. If not, we have some.\""
watchOutFor:
  - "Asking a question nobody wants to raise a hand for."
  - "Stacking three stats. One lands; three blur."
  - "Skipping the 'what to expect' part of the ask. People come when they can picture it."
tryIt: "Write your opening question for the next event you're going to. Test it: would you raise your hand?"

**The opening question** should be easy to say yes to and relevant to the room. For priority audiences, questions about change work well: changing jobs, learning something new at work, trying AI tools.

**Your story** is the same 30-second why you use in conversations. See Your Story.

**What The Labs is.** Say the one-sentence description as written on the slide, then point to the ladder: workshop, Build Cycle, Project Team, showcase, giving back.

**Proof** is one thing only. Pick from the approved set on the Customize page.

**The ask** names the next session. The deck reads it from the schedule, so it's never out of date.

**Join** stays up. Don't switch away from the QR while you take questions.
