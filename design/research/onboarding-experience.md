# The ambassador onboarding experience: research and recommendations

Researched 2026-09-27. Three inputs: The Upskilling Labs as it reads from the outside (its site, brand kit, press, and the OLOS repo); Red Antler's copywriting method (their case pages, Emily Heyward's interviews and *Obsessed*, and the critiques of the style); and a read of every screen in this repo, from the program page through `/apply/`, the guide, and the invite and nominate flows. Sources are linked at the end. Where a claim is my inference rather than something a source says, I say so. Nothing in the site was changed.

## The short version

- **The copy is already close to Red Antler's mechanics.** "A volunteer corps for people who can't keep a good thing to themselves", "The button says you're in. The pin says you passed it on", "Earned, not given", "That's it. That's the step." These are the real thing. The job is not a rewrite. It is to find the one truth those lines share, put it at the front, and cut the lines that don't come from it.
- **The experience has three finish lines and two names for the same journey.** The program page gives the pin to Mentors. The guide gives the pin for finishing five steps. The pin's own README gives it for finishing onboarding. The program page's "How it works" never mentions the guide at all, so an ambassador who was promised ten minutes lands on a page that says "Get ready. Five steps."
- **The ten minutes are in the wrong order.** The first screen after "Become an ambassador" is a form. The agreement comes before the applicant has learned anything. The video, the only thing that gives, is third.
- **The peak moment is a data dump.** "You're in" asks the new ambassador to text their coordinator a message with their ZIP, an agreement version string, and a quiz score. The evidence in the README says the dated personal ask is the strongest thing in the guide. It should be the first thing an ambassador does, on that screen.
- **Two real gaps in the experience:** an applicant without an invite link is "Almost there" until the coordinator sends them an invite link, which does flip them to "in", but no copy anywhere tells the applicant or the coordinator that this is how confirmation works; and "Mentor" is both the ambassador rank and an existing Labs role, and an ambassador meets both meanings within the first four minutes of the guide.
- **One ladder, four verbs, everywhere:** Raise your hand. Get in. Get ready. Pass it on.

## 1. The Labs, as a new ambassador meets it

What matters for onboarding, confirmed from public sources:

- **It is a real, singular, DC-born thing with a story worth telling.** The pilot was DC Public Library's free twelve-week AI Upskilling Cohort in fall 2025: 50 mid-career, mostly former federal workers chosen from 200 applicants, six pods, 35 volunteer mentors, co-run with Levy. The Urban Libraries Council reports 89% left more confident and 73% made five or more connections. The participants then started The Labs themselves; DCPL's Chelsea Kirkland described them as having "gone on to create their own community led initiative." That origin is the ambassador program in miniature: people who got something and passed it on.
- **It runs on a small, named vocabulary,** and the site enforces it with a linter: The Labs (never TUL), Upskillers, pods, Poderator, Build Cycle, Summit, Open Lab, "the commons", "Build Out Loud", learn by doing. "Problem owner", which the guide's scenarios and practice audiences use, does not appear on any public Labs page.
- **The brand kit already states the voice:** "Warm, Clear, Grounded. Build · Practice · Momentum · Together." Words to avoid: "Corporate, Hype, Jargon. Disrupt · Leverage · Ecosystem · Innovation." The main site's own lines set the bar: "Find your people. Build your edge." "Capability changes everything." "Not credentials. Not a profession. A mindset."
- **Two palettes exist.** The brand kit's "Civic Core" (Primary Teal, Action Red, Midnight) and the OLOS design system's warm-paper reskin (ink, teal, teal-deep, red, paper). This site uses the OLOS tokens, which is what the live homepage uses too. Nothing to change, but worth knowing if someone brings the brand kit PDF to a review.
- **The program is public only as this repo.** The Get Involved page lists volunteer roles (Mentors, Programs & Events, Communications, Participant Success, R&D, Governance) but not ambassadors. So the program page here will be the first time most people hear the word. It has to do all the work.
- **Scale:** the DC lab lists 184 members, with waitlists in Baltimore, Dallas, and Fairfax. The launch goal on the program page (60 ambassadors, 1,200 new Upskillers in six months) would roughly sextuple DC. That is either a stretch goal to say out loud or a number to soften; either way, "starting from 10" is the honest, likeable part.

## 2. What Red Antler actually does, and the part to skip

Red Antler's method, from Heyward and Osborne in their own words, is not an aesthetic. It is a sequence:

1. **Start from a human truth, not the product.** "The most important section is not what the business does. It's what we want people to feel when they interact with the brand." Casper's brief was not foam layers; it was "why do people even care about sleep? It's for how they feel when they wake up."
2. **Keep asking "but why does that matter?"** until the answer is an emotion, then ask whether that leads somewhere interesting creatively. Founders who describe a solution get told: "That's not a problem. That's a solution."
3. **Reframe the category by inverting its metric.** Hinge is "designed to be deleted." Ramp: "Smart is the new platinum." Keeps makes hair loss prevention "a point of pride."
4. **State the truth flatly, then hang everything off it.** Supergoop: "There's no skincare without suncare." USAFacts: "Opinions aren't the problem, opinions without facts are."
5. **Say who you're not.** "Do the hard work of figuring out not just who you are, but who you're not." "Brands need to be comfortable leaving some sets of consumers behind."
6. **Give the brand a character with a built-in tension.** USAFacts is "the passionate nerd, the numbers-obsessed truth teller that enlightens every conversation without convincing people to take sides." Tension pairs from the book: "earnestness and toughness," "rugged and goofy."
7. **Write short declaratives in second person, with the humor in the structure, not the punctuation.** "Best in bed." "Roll with it." "We Made It." "Furniture you want to move in with." "(you know who we mean)."
8. **For community brands, make the audience the co-author.** The Webbys' "We Made It" was written so "every nominee, winner, and fan" feels like a co-author. AllTrails: "The outside is in all of us."

The part to skip is the residue that copycats turned into "blanding": the made-up name in a sans-serif, the "affable but not overbearing, cheeky but not annoying" voice, "we're passionate about," "thoughtfully sourced," the "we're on a mission to" opener, and the founder-couldn't-find-a-good-one origin story. Heyward herself: "each time it's like a Xerox, it gets weaker and weaker." The test from GDUSA is the useful one: "If I close my eyes, I should be able to identify the brand just by listening."

The Labs' brand kit and Red Antler's method agree almost completely. The one thing Red Antler adds that the brand kit doesn't say is the *tension*. "Warm, Clear, Grounded" is a safe brief. The best lines already on the program page are warm and a little dry ("That's it. That's the step."), and that dryness is what stops them being bland. Name it in the brief so the next writer keeps it.

## 4. The truth to build on

Run the "why does that matter" ladder on the program:

- Why ambassadors? So more people find The Labs.
- Why does that matter? Because the people who need it most, the ones in the practice cards (just laid off, "not technical", "too busy"), don't find things through ads. They find them through a person they trust.
- Why does that matter? Because the moment someone hears "I was in the same spot last spring" from a person they know, that is the moment they walk in. The 30-second why is not a marketing asset; it is the whole mechanism.
- And why does that matter to the ambassador? Because someone did it for them. The founding cohort of The Labs literally is this: fifty people who got something and built a way to pass it on.

So the truth is not "spark curiosity" or "build community." It is: **somebody told you. Now you tell somebody.** The program is not a marketing channel with volunteers in it. It is the "I was in the same spot" conversation, delivered in person, by the only people who can deliver it.

Everything good on the site already comes from this truth. "Can't keep a good thing to themselves." "Passed it on." "It can't be bought, only passed on." "Bring someone in." The verb of the program is *pass it on*. The recommendations below just make the site say so, first and consistently.

The character, in Red Antler's form: **the friend who went first.** Warm because they were where you are; specific because they remember it; unbothered because nothing is for sale. The tension to keep is *generous and dry*. Never salesy, never "hey!", never a script.

And who the ambassador is not (say it on the page): not a recruiter, not a salesperson, not a promoter. Nothing is for sale. People join themselves. The agreement already says this in the negative; make it a headline.

## 5. Recommendations

### 5a. One ladder, four verbs, on every surface

Replace the three competing definitions with one sequence and use it on the program page, in the apply flow's step dots, on the guide's Home, and in the coordinator's messages:

| Verb | What it is | Time | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Raise your hand.** | The button on the program page. | A click. | |
| **Get in.** | Orientation: the short film or read, the five questions, the agreement, your name. | Ten minutes. | You're in. |
| **Get ready.** | The guide's five steps, at your own pace. | About half an hour. | You're ready. **The button.** |
| **Pass it on.** | [N] people join through your link. One talk. One new ambassador you bring in. | Months. | **The pin.** |

The one change of substance here is moving the physical button from "in" to "ready." Reasons: the guide gets a finish line that isn't the pin; the coordinator gets a natural checkpoint ("Maya says she's ready, send the button"); and the object marks the harder milestone, which is what makes objects mean something. The program page's "Ten minutes in, it's yours" becomes "Ten minutes and you're in. Half an hour and the button's in the mail." If the team prefers the button at orientation, keep it there, but then the guide must finish on "You're ready" with no object, and the pin stays with Pass it on. Either way, retire "finish five steps to earn the pin" from the guide and the pin README.

Also retire "Mentor" as the ambassador rank. A Labs mentor is an existing, public role (35 of them in the pilot), and the guide tells ambassadors to invite people with expertise to "guide a small team as a mentor." Let the object be the rank: an ambassador has the button or has the pin. "Bring one new ambassador in and the pin is yours" needs no title. If a title is wanted later, pick one that isn't already taken in The Labs' vocabulary.

### 5b. Reorder the ten minutes: give first, sign second, name last

New order for `/apply/`:

1. **The Labs in three minutes.** The video when it exists; until then, a short read on the same screen ("The short version") that the quiz is written from. Don't block launch on the film, and don't disable Next until the video ends. The quiz is the check.
2. **The five questions everyone asks.** The quiz, reframed (see 5d). Replace Q2 with the positioning question the scenarios already hold: "Someone asks, 'Is this like a coding bootcamp?'"
3. **The agreement.** Unchanged, now signed by someone who knows what they're agreeing to.
4. **Your name.** The form, last, framed as "so your coordinator knows who's in." This is the "sign here" moment and it belongs at the end.
5. **You're in.** See 5c.

The flow already resumes from the hash on reload and the invite link pre-fills the form, so the reorder is a content and sequence change, not a rebuild.

### 5c. Make "You're in" the first act, not the last form

The screen after the quiz should do three things, in this order:

1. **Show the object and say the true thing.** "You're in." Then one line: "Somebody told you about The Labs. Now it's your turn."
2. **Ask who they'll tell first.** One name field, saved into the existing asks store, with the existing text template behind it. The README's own evidence says the dated personal ask is the strongest thing in the guide; the strongest moment to ask for it is the moment they've just said yes. One name, not three. The other two come in step 2 of the guide.
3. **Say hi to a named person.** Replace "Text your coordinator so they have your details" with the coordinator's first name and face, and rewrite the message so it reads like a person wrote it:

   > Hi Ann, it's Maya Chen. I just finished ambassador orientation, so you'll have my details: maya@example.com, 20009. Sam brought me in. (Agreement v1, quiz 5 of 5.)

   The record-keeping goes in a trailing parenthesis. The version string can drop to "v1" since the coordinator can see the date.

Then the link into the guide, worded as the next verb: "Get ready" rather than "Open your guide."

### 5e. Names and words

- **"Ambassador"** stays. It is in the agreement, on the pin's backstamp, and Heyward's advice is that founders overweight names. What the program needs is a line, not a rename, and "Pass it on" is that line. Use it as the section title for Pass it on, the CTA on the share panel ("Pass it on: share your link"), and the last words on the pin's card.
- **"Mentor"** as a rank goes (5a).
- **"Poderator"** on the ladder needs a two-word gloss the first time ("Poderator, who runs a pod") or should drop off the ladder; an ambassador's audience won't know it.
- **"Problem owner"** is used in scenarios and the practice audiences but nowhere public. Either it is coming to the main site, or the audience label should be "Someone with a problem to bring."
- **"Upskiller"** gets one casing. The main site capitalises it.
- **"Orientation"** is fine and is already the word the flow uses. "Get in" is the verb for the ladder; "orientation" is what the step is called on the step dots.
- The linter's banned list should add "on a mission to", "passionate", and "thoughtfully". Those are the three bland tells the current copy is closest to.

### 5f. The human in the loop

The site can't see anything, so every moment after "You're in" is the coordinator's. Design those moments instead of leaving them to chance:

- **Name and picture the coordinator** on the program page's FAQ ("Who do I ask? Ann."), the done screen, and Home. `help.json` already holds the fields.
- **The coordinator sends the first message**, not the applicant, wherever possible. With an invite, the coordinator already knows who is coming. The done screen's "Say hi" then becomes a reply, which is a lighter ask.
- **Say how "Almost there" ends.** The mechanism exists: an invite link opened after passing confirms the person on the spot. Make it visible in three places. The pending screen: "Ann will text you a link. Open it and you're in." The coordinator's `/invite/` page: "Someone already passed the quiz? Make them an invite anyway; opening it confirms them." And the coordinator's text template for that case (below). If that feels like too much ceremony for a site that can't enforce a gate anyway, the cheaper option is to treat a passed quiz as "in" on the device and let the coordinator's reply be the confirmation.
- **Write the three texts the coordinator will send**, as templates in `invite.json` next to the ones that exist: "Welcome, you're in" (the confirming invite link, the guide link, and the first-ask nudge), "Someone joined through your link" (the first real win, and the one the site can never show), and "Your button's on its way." The second is the moment that keeps ambassadors ambassadors, and right now nothing in the system produces it.
- **The first ten.** "Recruiting our founding ambassadors" is true and good. The pin's backstamp already carries the year. Say "the first ten" on the program page while it is true; it is the most honest scarcity available.

### 5g. Smaller things, in passing

- The video screen's Next button should not be disabled until the video ends.
- Quiz Q5 asks for the exact legal phrase in the first ten minutes. Keep the phrase (it matters), but make the question recognition, not recall, and put the sentence on the done screen's "keep this" card so it is seen three times before anyone needs it.
- The time budget should be said once and truthfully, on the program page's "What it takes": ten minutes to get in, about half an hour to get ready, then "a few conversations and one talk" a month.
- The done dialog after each step is right; keep it. Add "First ask sent" as a visible milestone on Home once step 2's list has one sent name. It is the only win the site can actually see.
- On the program page, "What it takes" and the FAQ both answer the time question. One place is enough.

## 6. What to leave alone

- One path, one button. The README's deviations are right and the evidence behind them still holds.
- The story tool, the dated ask, and retrieval practice with a think-first pause. These are the substance; everything above is about getting people to them faster and in the right frame.
- The agreement's plain language.
- The pin spec, "Earned, not given," and the objects as the reward system. The program page's objects-as-milestones idea is the most Red Antler thing on the site.
- The design system, the no-pills rule, red only for "Join The Labs."
