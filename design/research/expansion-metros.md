# Detroit, Memphis and Milwaukee top the expansion shortlist

Measured by the one metric that describes a place rather than a state, the number of districts a city or county is divided among compared with the minimum its population forces, and then screened for economic distress and for library, campus and civic-tech fit, ten metros survive as core expansion targets for The Upskilling Labs: **Detroit (with Flint as a second Michigan site), Memphis, Milwaukee, Greensboro and the Piedmont Triad, Las Vegas, suburban Atlanta (Gwinnett and DeKalb, with Macon-Bibb as a satellite), Salt Lake City, Reading and Harrisburg, Austin, and Chicago**, in roughly that order. Detroit is the strongest case inside the seven swing states: **Oakland County sits in 6 congressional districts when its population requires 2**, the city is split between two districts although it could fit inside one, a federal court found in December 2023 that the commission's Detroit-area legislative lines were drawn with race predominating, and the city's poverty rate is **32.7%** ([Census relationship files, computed](https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/maps-data/data/rel2020/cd-sld/); [ARP, Agee v. Benson](https://thearp.org/litigation/agee-v-benson/); [Census Reporter, ACS 2020–2024](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B01003,B19013,C17002,B15003&geo_ids=16000US4263624,16000US4232800,16000US4287048,16000US4202000,16000US4269000,16000US4224000,16000US4261000,16000US4241216,16000US2622000,16000US2629000,16000US2670520,16000US2634000,16000US2646000,16000US5553000,16000US5548000,16000US5531000,16000US5566000,16000US5539225,16000US5522300)). Memphis is the clearest overlap of fracture and distress anywhere in the set, cracked from one congressional district into three by a map signed May 7, 2026, with **23 to 24% poverty** and the nation's largest county population loss in 2024, but it lies outside the swing states and its litigation is unresolved ([Wikipedia, 2026 Tennessee redistricting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Tennessee_redistricting); [Tennessee State Data Center](https://tnsdc.utk.edu/2025/04/10/shelby-countys-2024-population-decrease-is-the-nations-largest/)). Greensboro's Guilford County is the cleanest example of a county divided with no population justification (**3 districts for a county whose population is 0.73 of one district**), and Milwaukee pairs a county split beyond its minimum with 22.8% poverty and a congressional "packing" question argued before the Wisconsin Supreme Court on September 16, 2026 ([Common Cause NC](https://www.commoncause.org/north-carolina/articles/dra-analysis-2024-north-carolina-voting-districts/); [Wisconsin Law Journal](https://wislawjournal.com/2026/09/17/wisconsin-supreme-court-weighs-congressional-map-challenges/)). Phoenix, Raleigh, Charlotte, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, New York, Boston, Seattle and the Bay Area should be held: their splits are population-forced, come from commission or court maps with fair grades, or belong to affluent metros already served by active civic-tech groups. Two caveats govern every number here: the split counts were computed from Census relationship files by land area and must be confirmed population-weighted in Dave's Redistricting App before public use, and the Princeton Gerrymandering Project has not graded a map since October 30, 2025, so no official grade exists for any map adopted after that date ([The Fulcrum, Aug. 16, 2026](https://thefulcrum.us/elections/analysis-2026-congressional-redistricting-trends)).

## Split counts, not partisan grades, locate a broken metro

The measures the field uses fall into two families, and only one of them speaks to a metro. Partisan-symmetry measures such as the efficiency gap, partisan bias, mean-median difference and declination are statewide numbers: the efficiency gap takes "one party's total inefficient votes in an election, subtracting the other party's total inefficient votes, and dividing by the total number of votes cast," and PlanScore itself warns that only the efficiency gap and declination "should be trusted when one party predominates in a state" ([PlanScore, Efficiency Gap](https://planscore.org/metrics/efficiencygap/); [PlanScore, Partisan Bias](https://planscore.org/metrics/partisanbias/)). These numbers say how much a map's cracking and packing shifted seats, not which county was cracked. Splitting measures do the metro-level work: Princeton's report card counts "counties that are split into at least 2 districts" against a floor equal to "the count of counties that have populations larger than the ideal population size of a district," and Dave's Redistricting App scores how much counties are cut by districts and districts by counties, with 1.0 meaning no splitting ([PGP methodology](https://gerrymander.princeton.edu/redistricting-report-card-methodology); [DRA, Splitting](https://medium.com/dra-2020/splitting-7a3f295ec07f)). Because Princeton paused grading on October 30, 2025 and PlanScore's state pages render only in a browser (they returned "0 plans" to every automated fetch on September 29, 2026), the Census Bureau's district-to-county and district-to-place relationship files are the one source a volunteer group can reproduce end to end, and they are what this report's counts rest on ([The Fulcrum](https://thefulcrum.us/elections/analysis-2026-congressional-redistricting-trends); [Census rel2020/cd-sld](https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/maps-data/data/rel2020/cd-sld/)).

The screen applied here is therefore two-axis: a county's or city's actual district count minus the minimum its 2020 population forces (the excess split), and a distress signal (poverty, population loss, or the Economic Innovation Group's distress tier). Statewide grades and court findings are reported as context because they say who drew the map and how a neutral scorer judged it, which is the vocabulary all the nonpartisan groups share. The Brennan Center defines cracking as splitting "groups of disfavored voters among multiple districts" and packing as cramming "members of disfavored groups or parties into as few districts as possible," and the League of Women Voters' mapping guidance holds that "keeping communities in a single political district increases the ability of communities to influence or even determine who gets elected to represent that district" ([Brennan Center, Gerrymandering Explained](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/gerrymandering-explained); [LWV, No Harm to Our Communities](https://www.lwv.org/redistricting/no-harm-our-communities-mapping-guidance-elected-officials)). A sentence the Labs can reuse for any place follows directly: "[County] (population X) is divided among N districts; its population would require at most M. The map was adopted by [legislature, commission or court] on [date] and was graded [grade] by [scorer]." That pattern applies identically to Nevada's map, drawn by a Democratic legislature and graded F, and North Carolina's, drawn by a Republican legislature and graded F ([Las Vegas Review-Journal, Aug. 15, 2025](https://www.reviewjournal.com/news/politics-and-government/nevada/are-nevadas-maps-gerrymandered-3422121/)).

The land-area method has one known failure mode that the researchers flagged and that shows up inside this data. A district touching a sliver of a county counts the same as a district holding half of it. The Census file places Phoenix in six congressional districts (1, 3, 4, 7, 8 and 9), while the district descriptions transcribed from official lists place it in four (1, 3, 4 and 8), which means the intersections with districts 7 and 9 are almost certainly slivers where Phoenix's city limits brush Avondale-Tolleson and the far West Valley ([Census rel2020/cd-sld, computed](https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/maps-data/data/rel2020/cd-sld/); [Wikipedia, AZ-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona%27s_4th_congressional_district)). The same doubt attaches to Cary, North Carolina (4 districts by land area) and to Maricopa County (8 of Arizona's 9). Where an independent source corroborates a count, such as Common Cause NC's DRA count of 11 split counties in North Carolina matching the Census-file count of 11, the number can be used; where it does not, it is provisional ([Common Cause NC](https://www.commoncause.org/north-carolina/articles/dra-analysis-2024-north-carolina-voting-districts/)).

Ranked by excess congressional splits, **Oakland County leads at +4**, followed by Gwinnett, DeKalb, Guilford, Henry County (Georgia), Maricopa and Berks at +2, then a wide group at +1. Metro Detroit's case is unusual because its fracture is documented at both levels by different institutions. At the congressional level the Independent Citizens Redistricting Commission's "Chestnut" map, adopted December 28, 2021 by an 8–5 vote, places western Detroit with Dearborn in MI-12 and eastern Detroit with the Downriver suburbs in MI-13, even though the city's roughly 639,000 residents would fit inside one district of about 775,000; at the legislative level a three-judge federal panel held on December 21, 2023 that the commission "overwhelmingly - indeed, inescapably" drew seven House and six Senate districts predominantly on race, and the remedial "Motown Sound FC E1" House map (approved March 27, 2024) and "Crane A1" Senate map (approved July 26, 2024, first used in 2026) re-clustered districts inside the city, cutting the number of Senate districts touching Detroit from eight to four ([Ballotpedia, Michigan](https://ballotpedia.org/Redistricting_in_Michigan_ahead_of_the_2026_elections); [Wikipedia, MI-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan%27s_12th_congressional_district); [ARP, Agee v. Benson](https://thearp.org/litigation/agee-v-benson/); [Gongwer](https://www.gongwer.com/blog/155501/New-Senate-Map:-Partisan-Status-Quo,-Wild-Geographic-Changes)). The court's own description of the remedy, that "African-American voters will have markedly more power to elect their candidate of choice in 2024 than they did in 2022," is the kind of institutional finding the Labs can cite without characterizing motive ([Bridge Michigan, Mar. 2024](https://bridgemi.com/michigan-government/michigan-redistricting-court-finds-new-detroit-maps-better-black-voters/)). The maps are stable: no post-Callais suit against Michigan's lines had been confirmed as of April 30, 2026, and the commission's chair argued its "race-blind approach" would survive ([Votebeat Michigan, Apr. 29, 2026](https://www.votebeat.org/michigan/2026/04/29/independent-redistricting-commission-louisiana-v-callais-voting-rights-act/)).

Greensboro is the cleanest single case of a county split with no population justification. Guilford County's 541,000 residents are less than one North Carolina district, yet the map adopted by the General Assembly on October 25, 2023 (SB 757) places it in districts 5, 6 and 9, none anchored in the county; Common Cause NC's DRA analysis states that "Guilford County does not have to be split" and reports the Triad district's combined Black and Latino voting-age population falling from 36% to 24% ([Rhino Times](https://www.rhinotimes.com/news/guilford-divided-into-three-congressional-districts-in-map-expected-to-be-approved/); [Common Cause NC](https://www.commoncause.org/north-carolina/articles/dra-analysis-2024-north-carolina-voting-districts/); [Carolina Public Press](https://carolinapublicpress.org/73566/judges-uphold-nc-congressional-map-redrawn-by-legislators-in-2023-no-word-on-challenge-to-change-in-2025/)). The statewide scores frame it: Princeton graded the 2023 congressional map F and its director called the October 22, 2025 redraw a move "from an F to a bold-faced F"; Duke's Quantifying Gerrymandering group found nonpartisan ensembles "regularly elect 7 Republicans and 7 Democrats" while the 2025 map "largely locks in" 11–3 across statewide vote shares from 46% to 52%; DRA scored the 2023 map's proportionality at 0 ([WRAL](https://www.wral.com/princeton-research-group-fails-many-of-nc-s-proposed-voting-maps/19947776/); [Spectrum News, Oct. 21, 2025](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/nc/charlotte/news/2025/10/21/gerrymandering-north-carolina-trump-redistrict); [Duke Quantifying Gerrymandering](https://sites.duke.edu/quantifyinggerrymandering/2025/10/)). The 2025 redraw (SB 249) changed only districts 1 and 3 in eastern North Carolina, so the Triad, Raleigh and Charlotte counts stand; a federal panel declined to enjoin it on November 26, 2025, the Williams plaintiffs dismissed their remaining claims January 16, 2026, and one new suit, Gallop v. Hirsch, is pending ([NC Local](https://nclocal.org/2025/10/22/the-north-carolina-legislature-just-changed-the-voting-maps-what-happened/); [NC Newsline](https://ncnewsline.com/2025/11/26/federal-court-allows-republican-led-north-carolina-redistricting-plan-to-proceed/); [ARP, Williams v. Hall](https://thearp.org/litigation/williams-v-hall/); [All About Redistricting, Gallop](https://redistricting.lls.edu/case/gallop-v-hirsch/)). Raleigh and Charlotte are each in three districts (Wake and Mecklenburg at +1), but each keeps an urban-anchored district and both counties are affluent, which lowers their priority on the distress axis.

Milwaukee's fracture is a packing question that a court is actively deciding. The city (about 564,000 people, below the 737,000 district ideal) sits almost entirely inside WI-4, Cook PVI D+26, while Milwaukee County is parceled into WI-1, WI-5 and, by one account, WI-6, with Greenfield and West Allis each split ([Wikipedia, WI-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin%27s_4th_congressional_district)). The congressional map was the "least change" proposal adopted by the state Supreme Court on March 3, 2022 on top of 2011 lines; Princeton graded it F for "Significant Republican advantage," and in 2024 only one of eight districts was competitive with a median margin elsewhere of about 29 points ([All About Redistricting, Wisconsin](https://redistricting.lls.edu/state/wisconsin/); [PGP report card, Wisconsin](https://gerrymander.princeton.edu/redistricting-report-card/?planId=recAW6q19I516nHpc); [Wisconsin Watch, Oct. 2025](https://wisconsinwatch.org/2025/10/wisconsin-redistricting-maps-election-supreme-court-lawsuit-gerrymandering-democrat-republican/)). Two challenges were dismissed by three-judge panels on March 31 and April 28, 2026, the Supreme Court heard both on September 16, 2026, and plaintiffs asked for a ruling by January 4, 2027 with new maps for 2028 ([WPR, Mar. 31, 2026](https://www.wpr.org/news/judicial-panel-dismisses-lawsuit-challenging-wisconsin-congressional-districts); [Wisconsin Law Journal](https://wislawjournal.com/2026/09/17/wisconsin-supreme-court-weighs-congressional-map-challenges/)). The legislative side is the counterexample the Labs can point to: the 2024 maps were the first a legislature and governor of different parties agreed on, score at roughly 46 Republican-leaning, 45 Democratic-leaning and 8 toss-up Assembly seats, and Democrats flipped 14 legislative seats in 2024 under them ([PBS Wisconsin](https://pbswisconsin.org/news-item/democrats-flip-14-seats-in-the-wisconsin-legislature-in-2024-after-redistricting/); [Marquette Law faculty blog](https://law.marquette.edu/facultyblog/?p=30713)).

Suburban Atlanta has the highest concentration of over-split counties in the set but the least settled map. The 2023 remedial congressional map, drawn by the legislature after Judge Steve Jones struck the 2021 plans under the Voting Rights Act, took district 7 "completely out of Gwinnett County," which now touches districts 4, 9, 10 and 13, while Cobb spans 6, 11 and 14 and DeKalb sits in three districts although its population fits in one ([Capitol Beat](https://capitol-beat.org/2023/12/new-congressional-map-proposes-major-changes-in-atlantas-northern-suburbs/); [Wikipedia, Gwinnett County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwinnett_County,_Georgia)). Princeton graded the draft 2023 map C with zero competitive districts ([Princeton Election Consortium](https://election.princeton.edu/articles/data-showcases-weaknesses-in-proposed-georgia-congressional-map/)). On August 25, 2026 the Eleventh Circuit vacated the liability findings and remanded under Louisiana v. Callais; the 2023 maps govern November 2026, but "absent further injunction, the original 2021 plans would govern thereafter," and House Speaker Jon Burns declined to redraw in the June 2026 special session ([Atlanta News First](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/08/26/battle-over-georgias-political-maps-reset-after-court-tosses-out-earlier-decision/); [All About Redistricting, Georgia](https://redistricting.lls.edu/state/georgia/); [Newsweek, June 18, 2026](https://www.newsweek.com/redistricting-battles-us-georgia-declines-redraw-12089077)). Any Atlanta description the Labs publishes will need a 2028 caveat.

Las Vegas is the case that keeps the list nonpartisan. Clark County is at its floor (3 of 3), but the city of Las Vegas (660,400 people, under the roughly 777,000 district size) is spread across all three Clark districts, North Las Vegas and Henderson are each split in two, and the city sits in 16 Assembly districts ([Wikipedia, NV-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada%27s_1st_congressional_district); [Census rel2020/cd-sld, computed](https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/maps-data/data/rel2020/cd-sld/)). The map was passed by the Democratic-majority legislature and signed the same day, November 16, 2021; Princeton graded the congressional map F with all four districts outside its seven-point competitive zone, and at enactment the Nevada Independent reported partisan bias of 14.3% (Assembly) and 11.9% (Senate) favoring Democrats ([All About Redistricting, Nevada](https://redistricting.lls.edu/state/nevada/); [News 4 Reno](https://mynews4.com/news/local/new-nevada-political-maps-get-f-grade-for-significant-democratic-advantage-redistricting-princeton-gerrymandering-project); [The Nevada Independent, Nov. 16, 2021](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/after-redistricting-will-new-maps-give-nevada-democrats-permanent-majorities)). Defenders call the districts "compact and keep local communities together," and PlanScore publishes no Nevada scores by policy because the state has fewer than seven districts; both facts belong in any description ([News 4 Reno](https://mynews4.com/news/local/new-nevada-political-maps-get-f-grade-for-significant-democratic-advantage-redistricting-princeton-gerrymandering-project); [PlanScore, Nevada](https://planscore.org/nevada/)). The maps are stable through 2028; the only live activity is a repeated independent-commission ballot effort, one version of which was withdrawn in January 2026 ([KUNR](https://www.kunr.org/nevada-state-government/2025-09-24/nevada-nonprofit-files-ballot-petitions-to-change-primaries-and-redistricting)).

The remaining swing-state metros fall out for one of two reasons. Philadelphia (3 of 17 districts), Pittsburgh (whole inside PA-12, Allegheny at 2 of 2), Wayne County and Clark County are split only as much as their populations require ([Census rel2020/cd-sld, computed](https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/maps-data/data/rel2020/cd-sld/); [Wikipedia, PA-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania%27s_12th_congressional_district)). Pennsylvania's congressional map was chosen by the state Supreme Court on February 23, 2022 for its "least-change" approach and the lowest efficiency-gap bias of any proposal at −0.4%; Princeton graded it B and Fair Districts PA judged it "very near the center of the gray 'balanced' zone" ([Spotlight PA, Mar. 2022](https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2022/03/pennsylvania-redistricting-supreme-court-congressional-map-reasons/); [PGP report card, Pennsylvania](https://gerrymander.princeton.edu/redistricting-report-card/?planId=recT95YHHudtpR3Fc); [Fair Districts PA](https://www.fairdistrictspa.com/updates/pa-has-a-congressional-map)). Phoenix's Maricopa County is over-split by the raw count, but the lines come from a five-member independent commission whose draft Princeton graded A on partisan fairness, whose maps "were not subsequently challenged in court," and whose two swing seats (AZ-1 and AZ-6) were decided by under four points in both 2022 and 2024; the one threatened post-Callais lawsuit was abandoned on August 11, 2026 ([KJZZ, Oct. 12, 2021](https://kjzz.org/content/1724178/princeton-gerrymandering-project-likes-what-it-sees-az-redistricting-commission); [All About Redistricting, Arizona](https://redistricting.lls.edu/state/arizona/); [KJZZ, Aug. 11, 2026](https://www.kjzz.org/elections/2026-08-11/senate-leader-changes-his-mind-about-trying-to-redraw-arizonas-congressional-district-lines)). Phoenix's fracture reads as ordinary line-drawing plus a Voting Rights Act district (AZ-3, 62.6% Hispanic), so it belongs in a big-metro expansion plan rather than a broken-representation story. Reno and Washoe County are whole inside NV-2 and are not fractured at all.

## The mid-decade wave adds Memphis, Tampa, Austin and a repaired Salt Lake City

Between July 2025 and June 2026 ten states enacted new congressional maps outside the normal cycle (Texas, Missouri, North Carolina, Ohio, California, Utah, Florida, Tennessee, Louisiana, Alabama) and at least nine attempts failed or were reversed; the Supreme Court's April 29, 2026 decision in Louisiana v. Callais, holding that Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act "imposes liability only when the evidence supports a strong inference that the State intentionally drew its districts to afford minority voters less opportunity because of their race," drove the second half of the wave ([Wikipedia, 2025–2026 United States redistricting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_United_States_redistricting); [Justia, Louisiana v. Callais](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/608/24-109/)). Among the metros these maps touched, Memphis is the only one where a three-way crack coincides with sustained distress. The old 9th district was 60.2% Black with a Cook PVI of D+22; the map passed and signed May 7, 2026 (HB 7003) splits Memphis and Shelby County among the 5th, 8th and 9th, leaves no district with a one-third minority population, gives the new 9th a PVI of R+9 running 200-plus miles to Williamson County, and cut the 9th's median density from 3,075 to 789 people per square mile ([Wikipedia, TN-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee%27s_9th_congressional_district); [Roll Call, May 7, 2026](https://rollcall.com/2026/05/07/tennessee-passes-new-map-that-erases-states-lone-majority-black-district/); [Nashville Banner](https://nashvillebanner.com/2026/05/08/tennessee-congressional-districts-black-voters-memphis/)). A federal panel denied a preliminary injunction on July 23, 2026, finding no "meaningful evidence that race, not politics" motivated the map, and the underlying suit is pending ([Redistricting Online, July 26, 2026](https://redistrictingonline.org/2026/07/26/federal-court-panel-clears-tennessee-congressional-map-for-the-2026-primaries/)). On the distress axis Memphis's poverty rate was 24% in 2024 with child poverty at 31.3% and rising four straight years, the city has lost about 36,000 residents since 2010, and Shelby County's 2024 decline was the largest of any U.S. county ([University of Memphis 2025 Poverty Fact Sheet](https://www.memphis.edu/socialwork/research/2025-poverty-fact-sheet-updated.pdf); [Smart City Memphis, Feb. 2026](https://www.smartcitymemphis.com/2026/02/diagnosing-why-memphis-is-losing-people-and-what-to-do-about-it/); [Tennessee State Data Center](https://tnsdc.utk.edu/2025/04/10/shelby-countys-2024-population-decrease-is-the-nations-largest/)). Tennessee is not a presidential battleground, so the Labs must decide whether the criterion is "broken representation" or "swing state"; on the first, Memphis ranks first in the entire set.

Austin has the strongest institutional finding of any wave metro but the weakest distress case. Travis County went from five districts under the 2021 map to four under the August 29, 2025 map, with district 35 moved "completely out of Travis County" and district 37 shifting from a 49-point to a 56-point Democratic margin, and a three-judge federal court found on November 18, 2025 that the map "unjustifiably relied predominantly based on race" before the Supreme Court stayed that ruling on December 4, 2025 and, on April 27, 2026, kept the map for 2026 without reaching the merits ([City Cast Austin](https://austin.citycast.fm/local-civics/what-travis-county-redistricting-new-congressional-maps); [Wikipedia, 2025 Texas redistricting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Texas_redistricting); [Texas Tribune, Apr. 27, 2026](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/04/27/texas-redistricting-map-ruling-us-supreme-court-upheld-2026-midterms/)). Austin's poverty rate is 12.0% and the city grew to 1,002,632 by 2025 ([Census QuickFacts, Austin](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/austincitytexas/PST045222); [Wikipedia, Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas)). Houston (minority-opportunity districts from four to three, TX-9 moving from a 44-point Democratic margin to a notional 20-point Republican one) and Dallas-Tarrant (three to two) carry comparable map evidence and Houston was EIG's "most distressed city among the ten largest," but neither was profiled for libraries or civic tech, so they sit on a watch list ([Wikipedia, 2025 Texas redistricting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Texas_redistricting); [EIG, 100 largest cities](https://eig.org/the-economic-well-being-of-the-countrys-100-largest-cities/)).

Tampa was newly split three ways (districts 12, 14 and 15) by Florida's HB 1D, signed in early May 2026, with FL-14 becoming Hillsborough-only but moving from Democratic-leaning to a 55–44 Trump result on 2024 votes; the Florida Supreme Court declined to hear an injunction request on June 11, 2026 and a Fair Districts challenge continues in Leon County, where Judge Joshua Hawkes found "insufficient evidence of impermissible intent" at the preliminary stage on May 26, 2026 ([Jacksonville Today](https://jaxtoday.org/2026/05/05/congressional-districts-florida-changes/); [NBC News, June 11, 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/florida-supreme-court-rejects-challenge-new-gop-drawn-congressional-ma-rcna349602); [Axios, May 26, 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/26/florida-redistricting-map-desantis-judge)). Tampa's city poverty is about 15.4% and it is growing, so it is a fracture-led rather than distress-led candidate ([Wikipedia, Tampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida)). Cincinnati's OH-1 moved from a 53–46 Harris result to 51–48 Trump under the bipartisan commission map adopted unanimously October 31, 2025, which runs through 2030; the city's poverty rate is reported at 25.5% but it recorded its first population increase since 1950 ([Wikipedia, OH-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio%27s_1st_congressional_district); [Ballotpedia News, Nov. 3, 2025](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2025/11/03/ohio-redistricting-commission-approves-new-congressional-map-in-the-only-state-required-by-law-to-redistrict-before-2026/); [Data USA](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/cincinnati-oh); [Wikipedia, Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati)). Kansas City is a hold: the 2025 Missouri map split Jackson County three ways along Troost Avenue, but the Missouri Supreme Court ruled 7–0 on September 3, 2026 that a referendum suspends it, the U.S. Supreme Court barred reversion for November 2026 on September 25, and Proposition A on the November ballot decides its future ([STLPR, Sept. 3, 2026](https://www.stlpr.org/government-politics-issues/2026-09-03/missouri-supreme-court-referendum-congressional-map); [PBS NewsHour, Sept. 25, 2026](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/supreme-court-rejects-trump-backed-missouri-congressional-map-for-third-time)).

Salt Lake City is the wave's repaired case, and that makes it a different kind of target. The legislature's November 2021 map split Salt Lake County four ways and Princeton gave it "a zero for partisan fairness"; on November 10, 2025 Judge Dianna Gibson found the legislature's replacement "was purposefully configured to favor the Republican party" in violation of Proposition 4 and adopted plaintiffs' Map 1, which splits the county two ways with one compact northern-county district; the Utah Supreme Court dismissed the legislature's appeal February 20, 2026 and Map 1 is "locked in for the 2026 midterms," though League of Women Voters of Utah v. Utah Legislature remains active and legislators have floated a further constitutional amendment ([KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/50280892/utah-redistricting-congressional-map-splitting-salt-lake-county-4-ways-heads-to-gov-cox); [KUER, Nov. 11, 2025](https://www.kuer.org/politics-government/2025-11-11/utah-judge-picks-plaintiffs-congressional-map-over-one-favored-by-gop-lawmakers); [Redistricting Online, May 22, 2026](https://redistrictingonline.org/2026/05/22/utahs-court-ordered-redistricting-where-things-stand/)). A Labs chapter there would be building in a place whose residents just watched a court restore a whole-community district, which is a story about representation working rather than failing.

## Among the named big metros only Chicago and Miami carry fracture evidence

Of the metros the user named that have not already appeared, Chicago is the one with a full nonpartisan record. Illinois's 2021 congressional map, drawn by the Democratic legislature, received an F from Princeton "due mainly to its lack of partisan fairness" and non-compact districts; Harvard's ALARM simulation study found the enacted plan splits 32 counties against an average of 9 in simulated plans and yields 14.3 Democratic seats where simulations typically produce 11.9; PlanScore reports an efficiency gap of about 21% favoring Democrats; and the city of Chicago itself lies in nine congressional districts ([WGLT](https://www.wglt.org/illinois/2021-10-20/public-speaks-out-on-proposed-congressional-maps); [ALARM Project, Illinois](https://alarm-redist.org/fifty-states/IL_cd_2020/); [PlanScore, Illinois](https://planscore.org/illinois/); [Chicago Board of Elections](https://chicagoelections.gov/en/new-legislative-maps.html)). No mid-decade change occurred and the city's population fell 0.5% from 2020 to 2025 ([Wikipedia, List of U.S. cities by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population)). Miami-Dade's evidence is thinner and contested: the 2022 map survived the Florida Supreme Court in July 2025, and the May 2026 map reshuffled districts 24, 26, 27 and 28, moving district 24 north into Broward and south "to include part of Miami and all of Miami Gardens," with three Fair Districts suits pending after preliminary injunctions were denied ([State Court Report](https://statecourtreport.org/case-tracker/black-voters-matter-v-byrd); [Florida Phoenix, Apr. 29, 2026](https://floridaphoenix.com/2026/04/29/florida-legislature-passes-desantis-congressional-redistricting-map/); [ClickOrlando](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/politics/2026/05/05/3-lawsuits-now-filed-to-block-new-florida-congressional-map-after-redistricting/)). No court finding or retrievable grade characterizes the Miami-Dade lines.

New York, Boston, Seattle and the Bay Area show what the researchers called ordinary line-drawing. New York's February 28, 2024 legislature map was described as making "few changes" to the court-drawn 2023 map and giving Democrats a "minor boost," and the Supreme Court blocked a state redraw on March 2, 2026, keeping those lines ([City & State](https://www.cityandstateny.com/politics/2024/02/state-legislature-proposes-new-congressional-map/394483/); [Gothamist](https://gothamist.com/news/dems-get-minor-boost-in-new-proposed-ny-congressional-map); [Wikipedia, 2025–2026 U.S. redistricting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_United_States_redistricting)). Boston is split between MA-7 ("roughly three-fourths of the city") and MA-8 under a 2021 legislature map with no litigation found; Seattle sits in WA-7 and the majority-minority WA-9 under a bipartisan commission map whose only litigation concerned a Yakima Valley legislative district ([Wikipedia, MA-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts%27s_7th_congressional_district); [Ballotpedia, WA-7](https://ballotpedia.org/Washington's_7th_Congressional_District)). The Bay Area is the one place among these where the map changed: Proposition 50, approved November 4, 2025 with 64.42%, replaced the citizens' commission map with a legislature-drawn one for 2026–2030, and it was that map Princeton graded F on October 30, 2025 before pausing its grading entirely; the documented Bay Area effect is the regrouping of Pittsburg and Antioch out of a district with Richmond and Vallejo, and whether San Francisco's own CA-11/CA-15 split changed was not found ([Ballotpedia, Prop 50](https://ballotpedia.org/California_Proposition_50,_Use_of_Legislative_Congressional_Redistricting_Map_Amendment_(2025)); [The Fulcrum](https://thefulcrum.us/elections/analysis-2026-congressional-redistricting-trends); [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12063269/what-prop-50s-passage-means-for-the-bay-area-santa-clara-countys-measure-a-and-nancy-pelosis-retirement)).

Crowding matters as much as fracture in these metros. After Code for America ended its Brigade program in 2023, leaving "roughly 60 volunteer groups" to find independence, the survivors regrouped under the Alliance of Civic Technologists, whose member list includes BetaNYC, Chi Hack Night, Code for Boston, Open Austin, SF Civic Tech, OpenOakland, Open Source San Jose, Civic Tech Atlanta and Civic Tech DC ([Code for America](https://codeforamerica.org/news/reflections-on-the-brigade-networks-next-chapter/); [ACT, About](https://www.civictechnologists.org/about)). Code for Boston runs weekly virtual hack nights, Chi Hack Night elected its sixth board in 2025, BetaNYC's School of Data drew 600-plus registrations, and NYPL's TechConnect alone offers "more than 100 technology classes" free ([ACT, May 2025 newsletter](https://www.civictechnologists.org/2025/05/may-2025-newsletter); [ACT, April 2025 newsletter](https://www.civictechnologists.org/2025/04/april-2025-newsletter); [NYPL TechConnect](https://www.eventbrite.com/o/nypl-techconnect-15754426384)). The least crowded of the named metros are Salt Lake City (no ACT member found), Miami (Code for Miami's site still calls itself a Code for America Brigade with no current event dates) and Seattle (the former brigade now "operates as Digital Aid Seattle," a nonprofit-matching service rather than an open hack night) ([Code for Miami](https://www.codefor.miami/); [openseattle.org](https://openseattle.org/)). Library capacity in the big metros is not the constraint: Chicago's system has 82 locations with YOUmedia teen maker spaces "now available at all 81 locations," and Miami-Dade runs a single county system of 50 libraries with four YOUmedia sites, two YOUmake sites and a Technobus mobile lab ([CPL YOUmedia](https://www.chipublib.org/youmedia-teens/); [Miami-Dade FY2025-26 budget, Library](https://www.miamidade.gov/resources/budget/adopted/fy2025-26/library.pdf)). Seattle's capacity past 2026 depends on a $480 million levy put to voters on August 4, 2026, whose result the researchers did not find ([KNKX, July 29, 2026](https://www.knkx.org/government/2026-07-29/seattle-voters-decide-if-library-services-worth-property-tax-hike-levy)).

## Distress and civic infrastructure sort the fractured metros into tiers

The distress axis separates the fractured metros sharply. Among central cities in the swing states, the ACS 2020–2024 five-year poverty rates run **Flint 34.1%, Saginaw 34.0%, Detroit 32.7%, Harrisburg 30.5%, Reading 28.7%, Erie 23.3%, Scranton 23.0%, Milwaukee 22.8%**, against Madison 16.4%, Grand Rapids 16.9% and Kenosha 13.7%; Reading's bachelor's-or-higher share is 11.4% and Flint's 14.2%, and Detroit's median household income is $39,938 ([Census Reporter, ACS 2020–2024](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B01003,B19013,C17002,B15003&geo_ids=16000US4263624,16000US4232800,16000US4287048,16000US4202000,16000US4269000,16000US4224000,16000US4261000,16000US4241216,16000US2622000,16000US2629000,16000US2670520,16000US2634000,16000US2646000,16000US5553000,16000US5548000,16000US5531000,16000US5566000,16000US5539225,16000US5522300)). Population trends reinforce the split: Flint lost 22.2% of its residents from 2010 to 2024, Detroit 9.5%, Erie 8.7% and Milwaukee 5.3%, while Madison grew 22.3% ([Census PEP 2020–2024 cities file](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/datasets/2020-2024/cities/totals/sub-est2024.csv)). EIG's 2025 Distressed Communities Index describes Detroit as "a donut hole in the region" that "sits on par with Cleveland, Buffalo, Newark, and Memphis," and its 100-largest-cities work lists Cleveland, Newark, Detroit, Buffalo, Baltimore, Milwaukee, New Orleans, Memphis and St. Louis as the most distressed large cities while placing Atlanta in the "mid-tier and comfortable" range and Phoenix in the "at risk" quintile ([DETROITography, Sept. 8, 2025](https://detroitography.com/2025/09/08/map-distressed-communities-index-in-detroit-2025/); [EIG, DCI Stories: 100 Largest Cities](https://eig.org/dci-stories-the-100-largest-cities/); [EIG, 100 largest cities](https://eig.org/the-economic-well-being-of-the-countrys-100-largest-cities/)). Three of the four most-distressed fractured cities (Detroit, Milwaukee, Memphis) therefore appear on EIG's list independently of this report's split counts, which is the strongest available cross-check.

The North Carolina and Georgia counties divide into two economic tiers. Guilford (poverty 14.6%, median income $68,642, 39.0% bachelor's), Forsyth (14.5%) and Cumberland (16.7%, 27.8% bachelor's) sit in the middle, while Wake (8.1%, $105,768, 57.2% bachelor's) and Mecklenburg (10.4%) are among the most prosperous counties in the set, as are Cobb (8.7%, $102,738) and Gwinnett (10.8%) ([Census Reporter, ACS 2020–2024, NC and GA counties](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B01003,B19013,B17001,B15003&geo_ids=05000US37081,05000US37119,05000US37183,05000US37051,05000US37067,05000US13067,05000US13135,05000US13021,05000US13245,05000US13215,05000US13051)). Macon-Bibb (24.7% poverty, $51,234) and Augusta-Richmond (20.0%) are the distressed places in Georgia, and Bibb is also the state's clearest legislative-level example: the court-ordered 2023 map gave Macon two of five new majority-Black House districts drawn as strips into neighboring counties, and Bibb sits in two congressional districts though its population fits in one ([GPB, Dec. 12, 2023](https://www.gpb.org/news/2023/12/12/judge-ordered-5-new-black-majority-house-districts-for-georgia-macon-got-2-of-them); [Wikipedia, Bibb County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibb_County,_Georgia)). Las Vegas is a mixed case: poverty is 14.0% and the county is growing 6.2%, but bachelor's attainment is **27.7% in the city and 18.9% in North Las Vegas**, the lowest of any city the researchers checked, and in EIG's first index 45% of Las Vegas residents lived in distressed ZIP codes, a figure that is a decade old and needs refreshing ([Census Reporter, ACS 2020–2024, Nevada](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B01003,B19013,B17001,B15003&geo_ids=05000US32003,16000US3240000,16000US3251800,16000US3231900); [KNPR, 2015](https://knpr.org/show/knprs-state-of-nevada/2015-07-14/distressed-communities-make-up-one-third-of-nevada-report-finds)). Phoenix's distress is real but internal: EIG's South Phoenix case study describes a persistent-poverty tract group of 359,000 people, 31% poverty and a 2019 median household income of $36,800, almost entirely inside AZ-3 ([EIG, South Phoenix](https://eig.org/persistent-poverty-in-communities/case-studies/south-phoenix/)).

The fit signals then decide which distressed, fractured metros a launch kit can land in. Detroit Public Library's HYPE Makerspace already teaches 13-to-18-year-olds Raspberry Pi and Arduino programming, 3D printing and design, and the city hosts Wayne State University, University of Detroit Mercy, the College for Creative Studies and Wayne County Community College District ([Urban Libraries Council, HYPE](https://www.urbanlibraries.org/resources/makerspaces-in-libraries/hype-makerspace); [Wikipedia, Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit)). Flint's Gloria Coles Public Library reopened May 21, 2022 after a $30 million renovation built around a Digital Learning Hub, laptop checkout and three tech classrooms, funded by a $15 million Mott Foundation gift and a $12.6 million bond that 68% of voters approved, and Kettering University and Mott Community College are in the city ([East Village Magazine](https://www.eastvillagemagazine.org/2022/05/21/a-community-jewel-magnificent-legacy-community-leaders-celebrate-flint-public-library-reopening-after-30-million-renovation/); [Wikipedia, Flint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint,_Michigan)). Milwaukee Public Library opened branch makerspaces at Mitchell Street (2017), Good Hope (laser cutter, 3D printer) and Washington Park (IT, coding, robotics), and its Teen Connected Learning program has hired 30-plus paid teen interns; the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee, Marquette, the Milwaukee School of Engineering and Milwaukee Area Technical College are in the city ([WUWM, Dec. 30, 2021](https://www.wuwm.com/2021-12-30/milwaukee-public-library-promises-to-expand-services-to-teens-in-2022-including-more-makerspaces); [MPL makerspaces](https://www.mpl.org/library/makerspace/); [Wikipedia, Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee)). Memphis Public Libraries' Benjamin L. Hooks Central Library and 18 branches include CLOUD901, an 8,300-square-foot teen lab, plus JobLINC career services; the University of Memphis, Rhodes College and LeMoyne-Owen College are local, and Tech901 reports 2,790-plus students trained and 1,188-plus job placements ([Memphis Public Libraries, CLOUD901](https://www.memphislibrary.org/cloud901/); [Memphis Public Libraries, locations](https://www.memphislibrary.org/about/locations/); [Tech901](https://tech901.org/)). Greensboro Public Library runs a Central Library and seven branches, North Carolina A&T State University and Bennett College are in the city, and the UNC School of Government lists Code for Greensboro alongside Code for Charlotte and Code for Raleigh, though none of the three groups' 2025–26 activity was verified ([Greensboro Public Library](https://library.greensboro-nc.gov/about-us); [NC African American Heritage Commission](https://aahc.nc.gov/places/historically-black-colleges-and-universities); [UNC SOG, Civic Tech in NC](https://www.sog.unc.edu/resources/microsites/civictech/civic-tech-north-carolina)). The Las Vegas-Clark County Library District is the largest single institution in the set: 25 branches, 705 public computers, a $96.25 million budget, 31,192 programs a year, a Built from Scratch Lab and teen tech labs, alongside UNLV's 33,566 students and the College of Southern Nevada's 30,663; no volunteer civic-tech group was found there ([LVCCLD, Fast Facts](https://thelibrarydistrict.org/facts/); [LVCCLD, Maker](https://thelibrarydistrict.org/maker/); [NSHE Fall 2025 enrollment](https://ir.nevada.edu/documents/enrollment/enrollment_by_semester/Fall_2025_Final_Enrollment_Report.pdf)). Gwinnett County Public Library posts the highest circulation in Georgia (3,925,310 in 2025) across 15 branches, Cobb's 15-branch system has a makerspace at North Cobb Regional, and Civic Tech Atlanta is an ACT member ([Wikipedia, Gwinnett County Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwinnett_County_Public_Library); [Wikipedia, Cobb County Public Library System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobb_County_Public_Library_System); [ACT, About](https://www.civictechnologists.org/about)). Pennsylvania's distressed mid-size cities have smaller anchors: Reading Public Library has a main library and two branches, Dauphin County's eight-location system runs a MARCO mobile digital-skills lab, and Erie's Blasco Library opened an Idea Lab in 2017 with 3D printers and GED and computer classes ([Reading Public Library](https://readingpubliclibrary.org/pa-forward/); [Dauphin County Library System](https://www.dcls.org/services/); [Erie Reader](https://www.eriereader.com/article/erie-county-public-library-opens-idea-lab-to-public)).

## Blind check: North Carolina, recomputed from the blocks

Because Greensboro was named as an example in the brief for the North Carolina researcher, a second pass was run with no place named at all. It downloaded the General Assembly's block assignment files for the 2025 congressional map (SL 2025-95), the 2023 Senate and House maps (SL 2023-146 and SL 2023-149) and the 2023 congressional map for comparison, joined all 236,638 blocks to 2020 Census block populations and the block-to-place file, and computed population-weighted district counts for every county over 150,000 and every city over 75,000, with the minimum set at ceiling(population / ideal district) and the state's 5% legislative deviation applied ([NCGA block assignment files](https://www.ncleg.gov/Redistricting); [Census PL 94-171](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/decennial-census/about/rdo/summary-files.html)). The district populations reproduce the enacted plans to the person, and the counts match the Census relationship files for every top entry, which retires the land-area caveat for this state.

Ranked by excess congressional splits, then legislative: **Cary** (174,721 people; 4 congressional districts against 1 needed, with two of the four holding under 3% of the town), **Raleigh** (3 against 1, but two of the three are slivers under 1%; its fragmentation is legislative, 8 Senate districts against 3 and 11 House against 6), **Greensboro** (3 against 1, split 75/21/4%), **Guilford County** (3 against 1, split 49/33/18%), and **High Point** (6 Senate districts against 1). The top five collapse into two metros: Guilford, the most robust finding because its three-way split holds at any sliver threshold and is the one a federal panel described, and western Wake, where Cary's excess is real but the town's 5.7% poverty rate and the Triangle's growth put it at the bottom of the distress axis. So Greensboro's place on the shortlist stands without the seeding, and Cary is noted as a fracture without distress.

The pass also improved two fit facts for Greensboro: the city's own poverty rate is 17.0% against the county's 14.6%, the library's Central branch runs a Hands-On Learning Lab with 3D printing and monthly maker sessions, and Code for Greensboro merged into Code with the Carolinas in 2023, which is the one active civic-tech group in the state's fractured metros; Open Raleigh and Code for Cary are defunct ([Greensboro Public Library](https://library.greensboro-nc.gov/); [Code with the Carolinas](https://codewiththecarolinas.org/)). The reproducible script and per-district shares are kept with the research notes.

## Six data gaps must close before the Labs publishes any ranking

The researchers flagged gaps that change how confidently each entry can be described, and they should be stated wherever the Labs uses these numbers. First, **the Princeton Gerrymandering Project paused grading on October 30, 2025**, after grading California's Prop 50 map, when its founder stepped back from academic roles to run for Congress; grades attributed to Florida, Tennessee or Alabama's 2026 maps are not official Princeton grades, and the site returned HTTP 503 to every automated request on September 29, 2026, so even the older grades cited here for Michigan, Georgia's final legislative maps, Arizona's enacted maps and all state-legislative maps except Nevada's could not be re-verified ([The Fulcrum, Aug. 16, 2026](https://thefulcrum.us/elections/analysis-2026-congressional-redistricting-trends)). Second, **every split count was computed from Census relationship files by land area**, so a district touching a county sliver counts as a split; the Phoenix discrepancy (6 districts by land area, 4 by official municipality lists), Cary's 4 and Maricopa's 8 are the rows most likely to shrink under population weighting, which DRA's Analytics tab provides and which should be run on every shortlisted county before publication ([Census rel2020/cd-sld](https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/maps-data/data/rel2020/cd-sld/); [Wikipedia, AZ-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona%27s_4th_congressional_district)). Third, the 119th-Congress file predates North Carolina's October 2025 map, which is harmless for the Triad, Raleigh and Charlotte rows because only districts 1 and 3 changed, and the 2024-vintage legislative file does not include Michigan's Crane A1 Senate map, so Wayne County's Senate count of 12 is stale and should be replaced by the Gongwer figure of four districts touching Detroit ([WUNC, Oct. 21, 2025](https://www.wunc.org/politics/2025-10-21/nc-congressional-map-proposal-continues-to-advance-as-democrats-decry-impact-in-northeastern-nc); [Gongwer](https://www.gongwer.com/blog/155501/New-Senate-Map:-Partisan-Status-Quo,-Wild-Geographic-Changes)). Fourth, PlanScore's per-plan efficiency-gap and partisan-bias figures for all seven swing states and every 2025–26 map were unretrievable because the pages are JavaScript-rendered; the only PlanScore-derived numbers here are the −0.4% efficiency gap the Pennsylvania court cited and the 2.3% and 3.1% partisan-bias figures in the Pennsylvania commission chair's report ([Spotlight PA, Mar. 2022](https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2022/03/pennsylvania-redistricting-supreme-court-congressional-map-reasons/); [Nordenberg Report, Mar. 4, 2022](https://www.redistricting.state.pa.us/resources/Press/2022-03-04%20Report%20Chair%20of%202021%20LRC%20Final%20Plan.pdf)). Fifth, no numeric 2025 EIG Distressed Communities Index score for any city or county was retrieved, because the index lives in an interactive map and download, and the one county distress index quoted for Macon-Bibb (86.8, third in Georgia) comes from American Default Research, not EIG, and must not be labeled as the DCI ([EIG, DCI hub](https://eig.org/dci-hub/); [American Default Research](https://americandefault.org/)). Sixth, the poverty, income and degree figures are ACS five-year (2020–2024) rather than one-year estimates because the Census API required a key and QuickFacts blocked automated access; the 2010 population leg is missing for the large named metros; the Seattle levy result, the Maryland special-session outcome and the 2025–26 activity of Code for Greensboro, Charlotte, Raleigh and Miami were never confirmed; and no civic-tech volunteer group was found in Detroit, Milwaukee, Las Vegas or Pittsburgh, which may reflect absence or search limits.

Evidence for the top of the order is in the sections above; the ordering logic deserves one more paragraph. Detroit goes first because it is the only metro where fracture is documented by an independent institution at both the congressional level (the commission's own split of a city that fits in one district, and the most over-split county in the set) and the legislative level (a federal court finding and a court-approved remedy), where distress is severe by every measure retrieved, where the library already runs the kind of teen tech program the Labs' kit assumes, and where the map is not going to move before 2028; Flint, an hour away with the most distressed numbers in the set and a library rebuilt explicitly around technology gaps, gives the same network builder a second site. Memphis goes second on evidence and would go first if the Labs drops the swing-state framing: a single map turned one district into three in a city with a quarter of residents in poverty, but Tennessee is not a battleground and the July 2026 ruling is preliminary. Milwaukee edges Greensboro because its distress is far deeper (22.8% versus 14.6% poverty, a shrinking versus growing population), its library infrastructure is larger, and a pending Supreme Court ruling makes it a place where the Labs can document representation before and after a possible change; Greensboro remains the cleanest county-level teaching example and has two HBCUs and a listed brigade. Las Vegas belongs in the top five so the list demonstrably applies the same test to a map drawn by a Democratic legislature; its bachelor's-attainment gap and its enormous, well-funded library district are the fit story. Atlanta is sixth rather than higher because its over-split suburbs are affluent and its maps may revert in 2028; Macon-Bibb is the distressed anchor for a Georgia entry. Salt Lake City and Reading/Harrisburg are distress-first entries with modest or repaired fracture, and Austin and Chicago close the core list because their fracture evidence is strong but their distress is low or unmeasured and their volunteer fields are the most crowded in the set.

## Conclusion

The exercise changes what "gerrymandered metro" should mean for the Labs. The statewide grades that dominate news coverage are poor guides to place: Arizona's commission map scores well while Maricopa is the most-touched county in the set, Pennsylvania's court map scores well while Reading's county is cut three ways, and Nevada's F sits on top of a county split exactly as much as population requires. The county-versus-minimum count, corroborated where possible by a court finding or an ensemble comparison, is the metric that survives contact with the nonpartisan constraint, and it produces a list whose maps were drawn by Republican legislatures (North Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Utah 2021), Democratic legislatures (Nevada, Illinois), an independent commission (Michigan) and state courts (Wisconsin, Pennsylvania, Utah 2025). That distribution is itself the Labs' best defense of the choice of places.

Two implications follow for sequencing. The strongest targets are also the ones whose maps are about to be adjudicated (Milwaukee by early 2027, Memphis and Greensboro in pending federal suits, Atlanta on remand), so each launch should record the split counts on its start date and treat any later change as part of the local project, not a threat to it. And because the three most distressed fractured cities (Detroit, Milwaukee, Memphis) are also the three where no volunteer civic-tech group was found, the network builder's first task in each is to establish whether the field is empty or simply invisible to search, which is a question a library partner can answer in a week. Suggested order: Detroit with Flint, Memphis, Milwaukee, Greensboro, Las Vegas, Atlanta with Macon, Salt Lake City, Reading with Harrisburg, Austin, Chicago, with Tampa, Cincinnati, Miami, Kansas City and Houston on watch and the rest on hold.
