# The structure, in 3D: a plan for the diagram

> A plan, not a build. It proposes one 3D scene that explains the structure Brendan is proposing (`recognition-system.md`, and the `/structure/` page): how the levels, the workstreams and the ways in fit together for one person, and how the network grows from HQ at the core out to metro regions and their neighborhood and Main Street field teams. Sep 29, 2026. Nothing here is approved by the board.

## What it has to explain

Four things, in a way a person can see rather than read:

1. **The ways in.** Anyone can start. Make something, take a role, teach what you know, or back it as a Supporting Member. The doors are different, and one of them (dues) never leads to a pin.
2. **The workstreams.** Five major ones (draft): Build Cycle, Learning Pathways, Community & Outreach, Platform, Operations & Governance. Plus the smaller ones: events, communications, research. Roles live inside workstreams.
3. **The levels.** Contributing Member, Reviewer, Approver, Maintainer. Each level is *breadth*: how many workstreams you've had your hands on. One part, two major, every major, everything. Two people who wear the pin vouch for you. The pin is trust, in your region first.
4. **The network.** HQ at the core holds the commons and the standard. Metro regions around it run the programs, each with its own board, director and brass pins. Inside a region, field teams run the workstreams out at neighborhood Labs and on Main Street. New regions are founded one at a time by a network builder carrying the launch kit. Ideas travel the other way: piloted in a neighborhood, adopted by the region, sent upstream to the standard.

## The one idea: the pin, exploded

The pin already is the diagram. Its rings are the levels. What the pin doesn't show is *why* you got a ring: which parts of The Labs you worked in. So the scene takes the real pin (the same mesh and studio as the hero on `/contributing/`) and lays the workstreams around it as a disc. Rings appear when your coverage of the disc crosses a threshold. The doors are on the disc's edge.

Then it pulls back, and that disc turns out to be one metro region. Around a silver core, there are more of them. Same disc, same rings, brass. That is the whole structure in one motion: **the ladder repeats at every level, so the diagram repeats at every scale.** The org is fractal, and so is the picture.

Three acts, one continuous camera, scrubbed by scroll like the levels on `/contributing/`.

## Act 1: the disc (one region, one person)

**Geometry.** Camera starts nearly top-down, so the scene reads as a schematic. The pin sits at the centre, face up, in brass. Around it, at the plane of the pin's back, a flat disc divided into wedges:

```
                    Learning
                    Pathways
          Community  \    |    /  Build
          & Outreach  \   |   /   Cycle
                 ------ [PIN] ------
          Operations  /   |   \   Platform
          & Governance/   |    \  (OLOS)
                   (minor wedges, narrow, between the major ones)
```

- **Five major wedges**, wide. **Minor wedges**, narrow, between them (events, communications, research). Every wedge has the same depth from the outer edge to the pin.
- **Depth is practice.** Each wedge has two bands: the outer band is *trained* (you hold the role's button), the inner band is *practiced* (the metal rim on the button). A wedge counts as hands-on once its inner band is lit.
- **The doors** are on the outer rim of the disc, one per way in, marked with the object you get:
  - **Take a role.** A button-shaped door at the mouth of the wedge the role lives in. Ambassador on Community & Outreach. Upskiller, Poderator and Mentor on Build Cycle (Mentor also opens onto Learning Pathways). Poderator's door sits one band in, behind Upskiller, because it opens after a Build Cycle.
  - **Make something.** A door that isn't tied to one wedge: a small marker that can land on any wedge (a guide, a flyer, a bit of code go where the work is).
  - **Teach what you know.** The Mentor door, plus a marker on Learning Pathways.
  - **Supporting Member.** A button on the *outside* of the rim, beside the disc, not on it. It's a real door, but it never lights a wedge. That's the visual statement of "dues buy the button, never a pin."
- **The pin lights when The Labs runs on something you gave**: the moment any wedge's inner band lights and the marker for "we wrote it down" appears (a one-line citation, drawn as a small brass plate under the pin, the register).
- **The rings build from coverage:**
  - **Reviewer**, ring 1: two major wedges practiced, plus two small figures at the rim (the vouchers, drawn as two pin silhouettes) that light when the threshold is met.
  - **Approver**, ring 2: every major wedge practiced.
  - **Maintainer**, ring 3: every wedge, major and minor.
  - Each ring appears the way the existing `rankpin.ts` builds it (a band sliding out from behind the one before, with the sunray face), so the diagram and the real pins match exactly.
- **Active or emeritus.** After the rings, the disc gets a *seat*: a small chair-shaped marker on the wedge where the person holds their seat. Toggle it off and the pin stays, the rings stay, the seat dims. That's the "yours forever, the seat takes showing up" beat, and it's the only thing that ever dims.

**The one journey to animate.** One person walks in through the Ambassador door. Their wedge's outer band lights (trained, the button). Then the inner band (practiced, the rim). Then a marker lands on Learning Pathways (they gave a workshop). The pin lights, the register plate appears: Contributing Member. A second wedge goes hands-on, two vouchers light: ring 1. And so on, to three rings. Then they step back: the seat dims, nothing else does. One story, told by the disc, with the level picker jumping to any beat.

**What's deliberately not in Act 1:** coins (they carry no status and would muddy the disc), office pins (a committee seat is a different shape; it belongs in Act 2 if anywhere), and the tier names "Trained" and "Practitioner" (the two bands show it without the words).

## Act 2: the network (HQ at the core, regions around it)

**The pull-back.** The camera lifts and tilts to three-quarter view. The disc shrinks to a coin-sized brass disc in a dark field. At the centre of the field, a silver disc: HQ, the national org. The brass disc we were just inside is one of several in orbit.

This borrows the program page's orbit video (the orb with rings of networks, a pulse leaping node to node) so the two pages share one visual language.

```
                     · neighborhood Lab
              ·  ·         ·
           ·   [DC]  ·  ·     ← a metro region: a brass disc,
              ·  ·  ·  ·        the same disc as Act 1
                  \
                   \  (the launch kit, carried by the network builder)
                    \
        [ HQ, silver ]  ------- [next metro]
                    /
                   /
              [another metro]
```

- **HQ, the core.** Silver. It holds the commons: the standard, the playbooks, the platform, the brand. The core has its own ladder: silver Reviewers, Approvers and Maintainers, for the national upstream. That's shown by giving the silver disc the same wedges and rings.
- **Metro regions.** Brass discs in orbit. Each has its own board and director (a small office pin shape at its centre, the swoosh chevron, distinct from the orb). Each runs its own copy of the platform and playbooks: a faint line ties every region to the core, and a pulse runs along it each time something syncs.
- **Neighborhood Labs.** Small dots on the surface of a region's disc, fixed to places (a library branch, a community centre). They don't have their own wedges: they're points.
- **Field teams.** The region's workstream teams, drawn as lines that run *across* the disc from dot to dot, one per major workstream, in the wedge's colour. Where a line reaches a dot, that's a field team working at a neighborhood Lab. Where a line reaches the disc's edge and out into the street grid drawn faintly around it, that's the team on Main Street: problem owners, local businesses, partners. The lines are the grid: teams run across the region, neighborhoods are fixed to places. [PLACEHOLDER: confirm what "Main Street" names in the model, so the diagram draws the right thing at the edge.]
- **Upstream.** A pulse starts at a neighborhood dot (pilot), spreads across its region's disc (regional), and travels the line into the core (national). Same three stages as the `/structure/` page: try it, adopt it, send it upstream. Downstream, the standard and the kit flow out the same lines in the other direction.

## Act 3: expansion (metro to metro)

- **DC first.** The scene opens with only one brass disc, close to the core, and a dotted outline where the second will be. A caption: regions begin as steering committees inside The Labs.
- **The network builder.** A single bright dot leaves the core, lands on DC, and everything they do there is drawn as a growing stack of thin plates beside the disc: the launch kit. When the stack is tall enough, the dot leaves for the dotted outline, carrying it. A new brass disc forms there, with its first neighborhood dots and its first field lines.
- **Spin-off.** DC's tie to the core thins from a solid line to a faint one, and its own office pin brightens: The Upskilling Labs of Greater Washington, with its own board. The four spin-off criteria (a full cycle on its own, its own staff paid for, its own director, enough Reviewers and Approvers to fill the seats) are the four things that have to light before the line thins.
- **The pattern repeats.** The dot leaves again. The field fills with brass discs, all tied to the silver core, all built the same way. Last frame: pull back far enough that the whole network is a ring of brass around silver, which is the pin again, in reverse.

## Choreography and copy

Scroll-scrubbed, one section, with a picker for the beats (the same pattern as the levels on `/contributing/`: the stage holds while you scroll on wide screens, the picker jumps, and it's tabs over stills on phones). Roughly twelve beats. Draft lines, in the page's voice:

| Beat | What you see | Line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The disc, top-down, doors on the rim | There's no front door. |
| 2 | One door lights, outer band lights | Take a role. You get trained, and a button. |
| 3 | Inner band lights | Practice it for real, and the button gets a rim. |
| 4 | A marker lands on a second wedge | Or make something. It counts wherever it lands. |
| 5 | The Supporting Member button, outside the rim | Or back it. Dues buy the button, never a pin. |
| 6 | The pin lights, the register plate appears | Something you gave is now part of how The Labs runs. |
| 7 | Second wedge hands-on, two vouchers, ring 1 | Two workstreams. Two people who wear the pin. Reviewer. |

| 9 | The seat dims, nothing else does | The pin is forever. The seat takes showing up. |

## Where it lives

- **`/structure/`**, as the section after "The shape", replacing the static "Five kinds of work" grid: the diagram *is* the work, the people and the pin at once. The static grids that follow stay as the reference.
- A shorter cut (Act 1 only, beats 1 to 9) could sit on `/contributing/` in place of the current levels stage, since it's the same pin with the reason for each ring added. Decide after seeing Act 1 working.
- The deck (`/present`) gets the same scene as a 20-second loop for slide use.

## How to build it

**Live 3D on desktop, stills everywhere else.** The same rule as the pin: three.js on machines with a real GPU, a mouse and a wide screen, mounted when the section comes near; Cycles stills on phones, with reduced motion, and without GPU WebGL.

- **Reuse `public/join/pin3d.js` and `src/scripts/rankpin.ts`.** The pin, its studio lighting, its brass and silver, and the ring build already exist. Act 1 adds the disc (a lathed annulus split into wedges by a second material per sector), the doors (instanced small meshes at the rim), the bands (two annuli per wedge whose emissive strength is the "lit" value), and the register plate. Acts 2 and 3 add a second, low-detail disc mesh instanced per region, dots, lines and a pulse shader. Nothing needs a new renderer.
- **Scroll drives one number.** `scrubP` from 0 to 1 across the section, mapped to twelve resting points with a short hold at each, as `/contributing/` does now. Every animated value (band brightness, ring extension, camera pose, pulse position) is a function of that number, so scrolling backwards works and the picker can seek.
- **Blender for the stills.** Build the disc and the network as scenes in `LabsPin.blend` next to the "Button Hero" and "Cockade" scenes, render one Cycles still per beat at 1200 and 640 px, and ship them in `public/structure/`. The stills double as the design reference while the live scene is built.
- **Order of work:**
  1. Act 1 as a static Blender render (a day). This is the design decision: does the disc read?
  2. Act 1 live, on top of `rankpin.ts` (two to three days). The level picker and the journey.
  3. Acts 2 and 3 as stills, then live (three to four days).
  4. Phone tabs, reduced motion, and the deck loop.

## Decisions needed before building

- **Is a workstream the same as a role, or bigger?** The disc assumes bigger: Build Cycle holds Upskiller, Poderator and Mentor. If a workstream is a role, the disc has more, thinner wedges. (Open question in `recognition-system.md`.)
- **Where does Ambassador sit?** The plan puts it on Community & Outreach. Confirm.
- **Learning Pathways has no role button yet.** The disc needs a door there. Facilitator, Workshop Lead, or something else.
- **What does "Main Street" name?** Problem owners and local businesses at the region's edge, a corridor-level unit under a neighborhood, or a field team of its own. The edge of the disc draws whichever it is.
- **How many regions to show.** Three brass discs around the core reads as a network without looking like a promise. More would.
- **Whether to show office pins** (committees, boards) at all in Act 2. They're a different shape on purpose, so they can be there without confusion, but they're one more thing to read.
- **The minor workstreams.** Narrow wedges, or a single outer ring? Narrow wedges are truer (Maintainer means all of them); a ring is simpler.
